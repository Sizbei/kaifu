## Claim 1 — Foreign resident population

**Claim as stated:** "Japan has 4.12M foreign residents (record, 4th straight year, doubled in a decade)"

**Verdict:** **Confirmed** on the headline number and the record; **Corrected** on "doubled in a decade."

**Current figure + as-of date:**
- **4,125,395 foreign residents as of 31 December 2025** (令和7年末), up 356,418 (+9.5%) year-on-year.
- Immigration Services Agency's own wording: 「令和７年末の在留外国人数は、412万5,395人（前年末比35万6,418人、9.5％増）で、過去最高を更新するとともに初めて400万人を超えた。」
- Breakdown: 中長期在留者 3,858,499 / 特別永住者 266,896.
- This is the newest release (published 2026, in the 令和8年 press-release set). **The pitch's 4.12M is current, not stale.** Do not let anyone tell you otherwise.

**"4th straight year" — arithmetically true, but ISA never says it.** From ISA's own year-end series: 2019 = 2,933,137 (then the record), 2020 = 2,887,116 and 2021 = 2,760,635 (COVID dips), then 2022 = 3,075,213, 2023 = 3,410,992, 2024 = 3,768,977, 2025 = 4,125,395. Each of the last four year-ends set a new record. ISA's press releases say 過去最高を更新 but contain no 連続 phrasing — so this is your arithmetic, not theirs. Fine to say; just don't attribute the phrase to ISA.

**"Doubled in a decade" — this is wrong.** End-2015 was **2,232,189**. 4,125,395 ÷ 2,232,189 = **1.85×, i.e. +85% over ten years.** It doubled over *twelve* years (end-2013 = 2,066,445 → ×2.00). Saying "doubled in a decade" overstates by 15 points and is the single easiest thing for a Japan-literate investor to catch.

**Primary source URLs:**
- https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/publications/press/13_00062.html (令和7年末 — headline)
- https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/publications/press/13_00052.html (令和6年末 = 3,768,977, for the record-streak arithmetic)
- https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/content/001425981.pdf (国籍・地域別 在留外国人数の推移 table, 2012–2024 — source of the 2015 = 2,232,189 baseline; parsed locally)

**How to say it on stage:**
> "4.12 million foreign residents as of end-2025 — a record, and the first time Japan has crossed four million. Up 85% in ten years, and up 9.5% in the last year alone."

## Claim 2 — Students needing Japanese language support

**Claim as stated:** "84,759 students needing Japanese language support (record, doubled in 9 years)"

**Verdict:** **Confirmed** — and the number is fresher than the founder probably realises.

**Current figure + as-of date:**
- **84,759** 日本語指導が必要な児童生徒 in **public** schools, as of **1 May 2025** (令和7年度調査), published **25 May 2026**. Up 15,636 (+22.6%) on the previous survey.
- **Critical breakdown the pitch is missing:**
  - **外国籍 (foreign nationality): 73,313** — up 15,595 (+27.0%)
  - **日本国籍 (Japanese nationality): 11,446** — up only 41 (+0.4%)
  - **Including national and private schools: 88,045** (national 62, private 3,224). This is the first survey to cover national/private schools, so 88,045 has no prior-year comparison.
- **This materially changes the addressable market.** The growth is essentially *all* in the foreign-national cohort. Your household-level TAM is the 73,313, not the 84,759 — and it is the segment growing at 27% per survey. The 11,446 Japanese-national students are flat and are mostly returnee/mixed-nationality households where a Japanese-reading parent often already exists. Quoting 84,759 without the split makes your TAM look 16% bigger and 3 points slower-growing than the number that actually matters to you.

**"Doubled in 9 years" — nearly, but not quite.** The survey is biennial. H28 (2016) = 43,947 → R7 (2025) = 84,759 is **×1.93 over nine years (+93%)**. It *more* than doubled over eleven years (H26/2014 = 37,095 → ×2.28). Series from MEXT's own chart: H20 33,470 / H22 34,007 / H24 33,184 / H26 37,095 / H28 43,947 / H30 51,126 / R3 58,307 / R5 69,123 / R7 84,759.

**Three supporting facts from the same survey that are better than the headline number:**
1. **12,668 public schools** now have at least one such student — **39.4% of all public schools** (up from 34.1%). Two in five schools in Japan.
2. **7,301 母語支援員 (native-language support staff)** are employed by boards of education, up 1,035. MEXT defines their job as including 「保護者とのやり取りや保護者向け文書の翻訳」— *translating documents for parents.* **That is your product, currently being done by 7,301 humans, at public expense.** This is the strongest single stat in the entire evidence pack and it is not in the pitch.
3. Outcomes gap: high-school progression 91.5% (vs 98.9% all students); university progression 41.2% (vs 75.0%); non-regular employment among those who take jobs 49.6% (vs 6.5%). Use one of these if you want the stakes line.

**Primary source URLs:**
- https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/houdou/31/09/1421569_00007.htm (survey landing page)
- https://www.mext.go.jp/content/20260525-mxt_kyokoku-000049811_01.pdf (報道発表資料 — all figures above; extracted with pdftotext, parsed cleanly)

**How to say it on stage:**
> "84,759 children in Japanese public schools need Japanese language support — a record, as of May 2025, published three months ago. 73,313 of them are foreign nationals, and that group grew 27% in two years. They're in 39% of all public schools. Boards of education currently employ 7,301 native-language support staff whose job description explicitly includes translating documents for parents."

## Claim 3 — LegalOn pricing

**Claim as stated:** "LegalOn ¥80k+/mo enterprise"

The tiered figures circulating online (Growth ¥10k / Business ¥30k / Enterprise ¥100k per month) come from **third-party affiliate and SaaS-comparison blogs**, not from LegalOn. **Primary source URLs:**
- https://www.legalontech.com/jp/legalforce/flow (official pricing page — states custom quoting, no figures)
- https://www.legalontech.com/jp/faq/loc (FAQ — 1-year minimum term, pricing by consultation)

> "The incumbent AI contract-review player, LegalOn, sells to corporate legal departments on annual enterprise contracts with quoted pricing — no public price list, one-year minimum. That's a fundamentally different buyer and a different price point from a resident paying for one lease."

Say it that way. It makes the same strategic point (they are not your competitor, they serve legal departments not residents) without you owning a number you cannot defend. **If an investor asks "where did ¥80k come from?" and the answer is "a comparison blog," you lose the room.**

## Claim 6 — MEXT "new language-support model project"

**Claim as stated:** MEXT has a "new language-support model project" for boards of education

**Verdict:** **Corrected.** There is no new model project. There is an **existing subsidy scheme running since FY2013 that was expanded for FY2026** — which is a better fact than a new pilot, because it means the money is already flowing and the procurement path exists.

**Actual programme name and current status:**

**「帰国・外国人児童生徒等に対するきめ細かな支援事業」** (Detailed Support Project for Returnee and Foreign Children and Students)
- Running since **H25 (FY2013)** — not new.
- **FY2026 budget: ¥1,396,113 thousand (¥1.396bn)**, up from ¥1,153,593 thousand (¥1.154bn) in FY2025. MEXT labels it **拡充 (expanded)** — a ~21% increase.
- **Subsidy rate: 1/3.** Recipients: prefectures and municipalities (indirect subsidy for anything below designated/core city level).
- Funded menu explicitly includes: 「オンライン指導や**多言語翻訳システム**等のICT活用」— **multilingual translation systems are a named, budgeted, eligible expense.** Also 日本語指導補助者・母語支援員派遣 (dispatching the support staff whose job includes translating parent documents).

**Two adjacent programmes worth knowing:**
- **「外国人の子供の就学促進事業」** (since H27/2015) — ¥95,213 thousand for FY2026, flat.
- **「日本語指導が必要な児童生徒等の教育支援基盤整備事業」** (since H30/2018) — ¥12,259 thousand for FY2026, **down** from ¥18,348 thousand. This funds **「かすたねっと」(CASTA-NET)**, MEXT's portal of multilingual school documents. See competitive landscape — this is a free substitute and you need an answer for it.

**Primary source URLs:**
- https://www.mext.go.jp/content/20260126-mxt_syoto02-000046862_07.pdf (令和8年度予算（案）主要事項, 総合教育政策局, dated 2026-01-26 — budget lines and programme descriptions; extracted with pdftotext)
- https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/shotou/clarinet/003/001/1339531.htm (programme overview, H25年度～)

**How to say it on stage:**
> "MEXT's subsidy for foreign-student support was expanded to ¥1.4 billion for the fiscal year starting April 2026, up 21%. Multilingual translation systems are a named eligible expense at a one-third subsidy rate, paid to municipalities. The budget line already exists — we don't need a new one created."

# Competitive landscape

The pitch does not cover this. It needs to, because the honest answer is that **several categories of substitute already exist, one direct competitor launched two weeks ago, and the moat as stated is not yet proven to be unique.**

## Tier 0 — The free baseline everyone actually uses

**Google Translate / Google Lens camera translation.** Point-and-shoot Japanese→any-language on printed documents, free, offline-capable, already installed. Every third-party guide to living in Japan names it first. **DeepL** is the second default and is specifically preferred by residents for formal ward-office and legal documents because of output naturalness.

This is your real competitor and your real objection. Google Lens will tell a user *what the words say*. It will not tell them **what the document is, what they must do, by when, and it will not draft a reply at the right register.** That gap is the whole pitch and you must demo it, not assert it — put a real 学校だより next to a Google Lens output on screen. If the audience can't see the delta in three seconds, the pitch fails regardless of the TAM slides.

## Tier 1 — Direct competitors (both launched in 2026)

**NaviNichi.AI** (NaviNichi株式会社) — **announced 10 August 2026, seventeen days ago.** This is the closest thing to a direct competitor and it is not in the pitch.
- Positions as an AI platform connecting foreign residents with schools, companies and government agencies — covering pre-arrival procedures through employment and settlement.
- Claims **96% accuracy on official immigration form generation vs 61% for generic AI** — i.e. they are already making a "we beat general-purpose LLMs on Japanese official documents" benchmark claim. **That is adjacent to the founder's stated benchmark moat.**
- Traction claimed: 6,000+ registered users and 2 million cumulative visits within two months; 7 educational institutions and 5 companies in trials.
- Notable difference: it is an *integrated workflow system* tied to institutional processes (school attendance tracking, visa-expiry monitoring, corporate onboarding), **not** a camera-first decoder. It works because the institution is onboarded. KAIFŪ works from a photo with no institutional relationship. That is a real and defensible difference — but you have to know they exist to draw it.
- Source: https://trends.codecamp.jp/blogs/media/news-prt-186664-1

**TransHero** (合同会社EMYSTI) — **released 14 May 2026.**
- AI translation app for foreign residents with **six scene-specific modes: general, school, hospital, bank, immigration bureau, municipal office** — trained on administrative and legal terminology.
- Includes **camera translation for documents**, bidirectional simultaneous interpretation, and — directly overlapping KAIFŪ's differentiator — **"appropriate polite expressions output" tailored to each context.**
- Japanese/Chinese/English core with additional languages; sub-600ms latency. iOS and Android, pricing not disclosed.
- **This is the closest overlap with the register-appropriate reply generation.** The founder should download it before pitching. If an investor has seen it, "we generate a reply at the correct politeness register" stops being a differentiator mid-sentence.
- Source: https://www.mobilenews.jp/release/66073.html

## Tier 2 — Government free substitutes (the "why won't the state just do this?" objection)

**「かすたねっと」(CASTA-NET)** — MEXT's own portal, https://casta-net.mext.go.jp/
- Searchable database of **multilingual school documents** (多言語の学校文書) contributed by boards of education with heavy foreign-student intake, plus multilingual school terminology lookup and a multilingual school-calendar generator.
- Directly addresses the school-printout use case, free, government-run.
- **Its weakness is your wedge:** it is a library of *pre-translated template documents that a school might send*, searchable by a teacher. It cannot decode the specific piece of paper in a parent's hand, extract that child's deadline, or draft that parent's reply. It is supply-side; you are demand-side. Say exactly that.
- Note its budget is *shrinking*: the programme funding it dropped from ¥18.3M to ¥12.3M for FY2026.

**ISA 外国人生活支援ポータルサイト** — https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/support/portal/index.html
- 生活・就労ガイドブック, **8th edition, released 26 February 2026, in 20 languages** (incl. やさしい日本語, Vietnamese, Nepali, Burmese, Hindi, Ukrainian). 12 chapters covering immigration, employment, healthcare, education, tax, housing. Plus orientation videos and a plain-Japanese conversion tool.
- Again: a static guidebook explaining procedures in general. Not a decoder for the document in front of you.

**Municipal counter interpretation services** — well-funded and widely deployed:
- **VoiceBiz®** (TOPPAN) — voice translation in 13 languages, text in 30; deployed at **120+ municipalities, educational institutions and ministries.** Ayase City deployed it across 5 elementary and 1 junior high school specifically for 国際教室 instruction and **parent communication.**
- **KOTOBAL** (Konica Minolta) — tablet-based interpretation in **32 languages** for municipal, hotel and financial counters, explicitly including **parent-teacher meetings with foreign-national guardians.** Kawaguchi City deployed 25 units in 2023.
- **やさしい日本語 AI conversion** — Hitachi Social Information Services, Alphasado 「伝えるウェブ」 (free for municipalities/schools/medical through March 2026), 「やさにちリライト＋」.
- These solve the *live conversation at the counter*. They do not solve the *envelope that arrived on Tuesday*. Different moment, different product — but a municipal buyer with a KOTOBAL contract will ask you why they need both.

## Tier 3 — Adjacent players in the pitch's named categories

**Rental contract checking (賃貸):** There is an active AI contract-check market — Ai-Smart重説 (auto-generates 重要事項説明書 and lease agreements, 240 min → 10 min), A.N LAB's cross-document verification, and a broad AI契約書レビュー category. **Every one of these sells to the real estate agency, not the tenant.** They check the landlord's paperwork for the landlord's benefit. Nobody in this list is checking a lease *on behalf of the foreign tenant*. That is a genuine gap.

The public baseline for "is this clause normal?" already exists in a form nobody has operationalised: **MLIT's 賃貸住宅標準契約書** (standard residential lease, most recently revised January 2025) and 定期賃貸住宅標準契約書. These are the government's model lease terms — the literal definition of a normal clause. I found no foreign-language version published by MLIT.
- https://www.mlit.go.jp/jutakukentiku/house/jutakukentiku_house_tk3_000023.html
- https://www.mlit.go.jp/jutakukentiku/house/jutakukentiku_house_tk3_000030.html

**YOLO JAPAN** — ~400,000 registered foreign-resident members from 246 countries (as of June 2026), founded 2004. Jobs board (ヨロワーク), market research (YOLO SURVEY), multilingual medical questionnaires (YOLO MEDICAL), and residence-status change/renewal filing assistance. Recently launched a recruitment system at ¥3,000/month. **Distribution, not product** — they own the audience you want and sell them nothing that decodes documents. Partnership target.
- https://www.yolo-japan.co.jp/

**MyVisaJapan** — **could not verify.** No primary source found for a distinctly branded "MyVisaJapan" service or any scale figures. Do not name it on stage.

**LO-PAL** — a marketplace matching foreign residents with local Japanese helpers, in 9 languages (EN/ZH/VI/PT/KO/NE/TL/ID/ES). Its explicit use cases are *exactly yours*: "explanation of city hall letters, help drafting messages to schools or landlords," rental contract terms, school consent forms. Its own marketing concedes Google Translate and DeepL are "Plan A" and positions itself as the human "Plan B" for "tone, nuance, keigo, or what does this clause mean for me?" **That sentence is KAIFŪ's product spec, written by someone selling humans to do it.** Strong validation of the problem; strong warning that the framing is already in market.
- https://lo-pal.app/guide/en/translation-apps-japan-2026

**Linc** — "Global Hub Talent," launched January 2026, automates foreign-employee labour management with **AI auto-generation of immigration filing documents.** B2B/employer-side. Overlaps the workplace-document half of the market.

## Is anyone already building the stated moat?

The moat as stated is (a) a corpus of decoded documents and (b) an "is this clause normal?" benchmark. Honestly:

**(a) The corpus — partially yes, and by well-resourced parties.** MEXT's かすたねっと is a state-funded, growing corpus of real school documents in multiple languages, contributed by the boards of education with the most foreign students. It is a *translated-template* corpus rather than a *decoded* one, and it is not structured for AI evaluation — but if MEXT decides to expose it as a dataset, a large chunk of your school-side corpus advantage evaporates overnight. On the ward-office side, ISA's 20-language guidebook is a similar public asset. Your defensible corpus is the part neither has: **real received documents with the actual action, actual deadline, and the reply that was actually sent** — outcome-linked, not translation-linked. That framing survives contact with かすたねっと. "We have a corpus of Japanese documents" does not.

**(b) The benchmark — someone is already making the claim.** NaviNichi.AI is publicly asserting 96% vs 61% accuracy against generic AI on official immigration forms, seventeen days ago. That is a benchmark claim in your category, from a funded-looking competitor, staked before you. Nobody appears to have built the specific thing — an "is this clause normal?" evaluation for Japanese residential leases graded against MLIT's standard lease — and that specific artefact is genuinely novel and genuinely defensible. **But it does not exist yet, and "we will build a benchmark" is not a moat.** If you want it to hold on stage, build the first 200 clauses against the MLIT standard lease and show the grader. The raw material is free and public.

**Bottom line to say if asked "what stops Google?":** Google will always win at rendering the characters into your language. It will never know that this particular 通知 means your child's 就学時健康診断 is in eleven days, that non-attendance triggers a follow-up from the board of education, and that the reply should be 丁寧語 not 敬語 because it goes to a teacher rather than the ward office. Then show it, don't say it.

**1. "LegalOn ¥80k+/mo enterprise" — drop the number entirely.** LegalOn publishes no pricing anywhere; its own page says pricing is quoted individually per customer. The tiered figures online come from affiliate comparison blogs, and none of them says ¥80k. This is an unsourceable number about a named third party's commercial terms — the worst category. ## The single most important correction

**Drop the LegalOn ¥80k figure.** It is the only claim in the pitch with no primary source of any kind, it concerns a named company's commercial terms, and it is the one an investor is most likely to test — LegalOn is a well-known Japanese legal-tech company and anyone in the room who has bought from them knows their pricing is quoted, not listed.
